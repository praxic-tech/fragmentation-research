Ran the LLM adversary on Michael's prose corpus. Results are in, both content
types scored. Here's the full picture:

IDENTIFIER DATA (50 records, 4 fields each)

  Game O tau   : 0.2133   vs  Memon-Greedy 0.48 | Memon-MST 0.36 | random ~0.00
  Game L F1    : 0.0133   vs  Memon-Greedy 0.030 | Memon-MST 0.030 | random ~0.02

  LLM is above random on ordering but below Memon. Linkage sits right on the
  random floor — a frontier model, IDs masked, given all 200 fragments at once,
  cannot re-link a name to its SSN. Nothing in the content connects them.

PROSE CORPUS (25 records, 3-6 sentences each)

  Game O tau   : 0.9733   vs  Memon-Greedy 0.72 | Memon-MST 0.55 | random ~0.00
  Game L F1    : 0.8921   vs  Memon-Greedy 0.046 | Memon-MST 0.046 | random ~0.03

  LLM dominates both games on prose. Ordering near-perfect (0.97), linkage at
  0.89. Clinical notes have real narrative structure — patient history into
  symptoms into diagnosis into treatment — and the model follows it cleanly.
  Memon barely moves on linkage (0.046) while the LLM hits 0.89. That gap is
  the finding.

THE CONTRAST IS THE THESIS

  Identifiers: fragmentation holds. LLM at chance on both games.
  Prose: fragmentation fails. LLM recovers ordering and linkage near-perfectly.

  The difference is not model capability — it's whether the content carries
  recoverable structure. Identifiers do not. Clinical prose does. This is
  exactly the content-dependence hypothesis, confirmed from the LLM side on
  both content types.
