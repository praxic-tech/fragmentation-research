## How the estimates were made, and their limits
- **Input tokens:** a linear fit of billed input tokens on prompt characters over the 249 of our 281 completed one-shot J answers that needed no repair (a repair adds a second prompt to the unit's billed input) (tokens ≈ 290 + 0.370 × chars). Token prompts carry one label per word, so their per-character rate is uncertain; input is under 1% of the cost either way.
- **Output tokens:** a log-log fit of the answering call's output tokens on fragment count over the same 281 answers (out ≈ e^5.71 × n^0.98), with each pool's uncertainty drawn from the fit's empirical residuals and capped at 128k (20,000 draws, seed 42).
- **Historical anchors:** of our one-shot J units with 230–330 fragments, 103 were attempted, 1 was a non-completion, 12 of the completed ones used ≥ 100k output tokens, and their median was 83290. Units with ≤ 40 fragments (the sentence condition's scale): 20, median output 7588.
- **The main caveat:** every historical unit had sentence-, clause- or 12-word-window fragments. Single-token fragments are a new regime: the model may reason more per fragment (more output, higher non-completion risk) or give up sooner. The fit is the best empirical anchor we have, not a validated model for this regime, so the worst-case line is the budget to plan against.
- **Non-completion risk** (per token pool, in the table): the share of the fit's residuals that would push that pool past 128k. RBNCs are kept as RBNCs under D6, never replaced.
